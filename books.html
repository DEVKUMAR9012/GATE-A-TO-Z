GATE CS Book Arsenal

The only books you need for GATE CS, with real covers, community ratings, and the exact chapters that matter. Don't read cover-to-cover — use the chapter guides below.

⚠️ Golden Rule No GATE topper reads books cover-to-cover. Identify the high-yield chapters, study them deeply, then immediately practice PYQs on that topic. Repeat. Books are a reference, not a syllabus.

Detailed Book Cards

CLRS
Algorithms • 10–13 Marks

Introduction to Algorithms

Thomas H. Cormen, Charles Leiserson, Ronald Rivest, Clifford Stein

3rd Edition

4.4/5 (28,000+ ratings)
Must Have 🔥
Key Chapters for GATE

Ch. 2–4 (Sorting), Ch. 6 (Heaps), Ch. 15 (DP), Ch. 16 (Greedy), Ch. 22–24 (Graph Algorithms)

Don't read proofs — understand the algorithms and time complexity derivations. Solve end-of-chapter problems.
Find on Amazon
Horowitz & Sahni
Data Structures • 8–10 Marks

Fundamentals of Data Structures in C

Ellis Horowitz, Sartaj Sahni, Susan Anderson-Freed

2nd Edition

4.1/5 (5,200+ ratings)
Recommended
Key Chapters for GATE

Ch. 3 (Stacks/Queues), Ch. 4 (Linked Lists), Ch. 5 (Trees), Ch. 6 (Graphs), Ch. 7 (Sorting)

Also supplement with Mark Allen Weiss for AVL trees and hashing. Both together cover all DS GATE questions.
Find on Amazon
Rosen
Discrete Mathematics • 8–10 Marks

Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications

Kenneth H. Rosen

7th Edition

3.9/5 (9,800+ ratings)
Recommended
Key Chapters for GATE

Ch. 1 (Logic/Propositions), Ch. 5 (Induction), Ch. 8 (Relations), Ch. 10–11 (Graph Theory), Ch. 6 (Combinatorics)

For probability, use Sheldon Ross 'Introduction to Probability Models' Ch. 1–4 as a supplement.
Find on Amazon
Peter Linz
Theory of Computation • 6–8 Marks

An Introduction to Formal Languages and Automata

Peter Linz

5th Edition

4.0/5 (3,400+ ratings)
Best for GATE
Key Chapters for GATE

Ch. 2–3 (DFA, NFA, Regex), Ch. 4–5 (CFG, PDA), Ch. 8–9 (TM, Undecidability)

Linz is easier to understand than Hopcroft/Ullman for GATE purposes. Use Hopcroft only for very specific proofs.
Find on Amazon
Dragon Book
Compiler Design • 5–7 Marks

Compilers: Principles, Techniques, and Tools

Alfred V. Aho, Monica S. Lam, Ravi Sethi, Jeffrey D. Ullman

2nd Edition

4.0/5 (11,500+ ratings)
Selective Chapters
Key Chapters for GATE

Ch. 2 (Lexical Analysis), Ch. 4 (Parsing — LL, LR, SLR, LALR), Ch. 5 (Syntax Directed Translation)

This book is very dense. For GATE, focus only on parsing sections. Lecture slides from Ullman are simpler and freely available online.
Find on Amazon
Morris Mano
Digital Logic • 5–6 Marks

Digital Design

M. Morris Mano

4th Edition

4.2/5 (7,600+ ratings)
Must Have 🔥
Key Chapters for GATE

Ch. 1 (Number Systems), Ch. 2 (Boolean Algebra), Ch. 3 (K-Maps), Ch. 4 (Combinational Circuits), Ch. 6 (Sequential Circuits), Ch. 8 (Registers)

Solve ALL the K-map problems at end of chapters. Combinational and Sequential circuit questions repeat heavily in GATE.

Carl Hamacher
Computer Organization (COA) • 5–7 Marks

Computer Organization and Embedded Systems

Carl Hamacher, Zvonko Vranesic, Safwat Zaky, Naraig Manjikian

6th Edition

3.9/5 (4,100+ ratings)
Recommended
Key Chapters for GATE

Ch. 5 (CPU Design), Ch. 6 (Pipelining), Ch. 8 (Memory Hierarchy/Cache), Ch. 9 (I/O — DMA, Interrupts)

Cache memory questions (mapping, replacement policies, hit ratio) are numerical-heavy. Practice at least 30 cache problems.
Find on Amazon
Dinosaur Book
Operating Systems • 8–10 Marks

Operating System Concepts

Abraham Silberschatz, Peter B. Galvin, Greg Gagne

9th Edition

4.1/5 (16,200+ ratings)
Must Have 🔥
Key Chapters for GATE

Ch. 5 (CPU Scheduling), Ch. 6 (Process Synchronization), Ch. 7 (Deadlocks), Ch. 8–9 (Memory: Paging, Segmentation, Virtual Memory), Ch. 12 (I/O)

Every CPU scheduling algorithm (FCFS, SJF, Round Robin, Priority) has been asked in GATE multiple times. Practice Gantt charts until they're effortless.

Korth & Sudarshan
Databases (DBMS) • 8–10 Marks

Database System Concepts

Abraham Silberschatz, Henry F. Korth, S. Sudarshan

6th Edition

4.0/5 (8,900+ ratings)
Must Have 🔥
Key Chapters for GATE

Ch. 2–4 (Relational Model, SQL), Ch. 7 (Normalization 1NF–BCNF), Ch. 11 (Indexing, B/B+ Trees), Ch. 14–15 (Transactions, Concurrency)

Normalization (finding candidate keys, decomposition) is a guaranteed 2-mark question every year. Master functional dependencies inside out.

Forouzan
Computer Networks • 8–10 Marks

Data Communications and Networking

Behrouz A. Forouzan

5th Edition

4.0/5 (12,400+ ratings)
Must Have 🔥
Key Chapters for GATE

Ch. 3 (Data Link: Error Detection), Ch. 5 (Network/IP/Subnetting), Ch. 6 (Transport/TCP/UDP), Ch. 7 (Application: DNS, HTTP, FTP), Ch. 12 (Routing Protocols)

Subnetting and IP addressing is mandatory. Also Kurose & Ross 'Top-Down Approach' is a great alternate — many prefer it for TCP/congestion control.

Higher Engineering Mathematics

B.S. Grewal

44th Edition

4.3/5 (14,700+ ratings)
India's Favourite
Key Chapters for GATE

Unit 4 (Probability & Statistics), Unit 5 (Complex Variables — skip for GATE usually), Ch. on Matrices and Linear Algebra (very important for GATE)

Grewal is a supplementary reference. For GATE Engg. Math (Discrete Math focus), Rosen's Discrete Math is more targeted. Use Grewal for Calculus, Probability, and Linear Algebra only.
