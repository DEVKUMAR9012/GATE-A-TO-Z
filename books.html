The GATE CS Book Arsenal

The definitive guide to which books to use for each subject, what chapters to focus on, and exactly how much of the book you actually need for GATE.

⚠️ Don't Read Books Cover-to-Cover No GATE topper reads an entire textbook. Focus on selected chapters only. The weightage column tells you how many marks each subject is worth — prioritize accordingly.
Algorithms · ~10–13 Marks
Introduction to Algorithms (CLRS)
Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest, Stein
📌 Focus: Ch. 2-4 (Sorting, Recurrence), Ch. 15 (DP), Ch. 16 (Greedy), Ch. 22-24 (Graphs). Skip proofs — focus on understanding and time complexity derivations.
Must Have★★★★★
Data Structures · ~8–10 Marks
Data Structures & Algorithm Analysis in C
Mark Allen Weiss
📌 Focus: Trees (Ch. 4), Hashing (Ch. 5), Graphs (Ch. 9). Very well-written for GATE. Excellent for BST, AVL, and Heap questions.
Recommended★★★★☆
Operating Systems · ~8–10 Marks
Operating System Concepts (Dinosaur Book)
Silberschatz, Galvin, Gagne
📌 Focus: Ch. 5 (CPU Scheduling), Ch. 6 (Synchronization), Ch. 7 (Deadlocks), Ch. 8-9 (Memory Management). Most GATE OS questions come directly from these chapters.
Must Have★★★★☆
DBMS · ~8–10 Marks
Database System Concepts
Korth, Silberschatz, Sudarshan
📌 Focus: Ch. 2-4 (Relational Model, SQL), Ch. 7 (Normalization), Ch. 15 (Indexing & B Trees), Ch. 17-18 (Transactions). Skip Ch. 20+ (Advanced topics rarely appear).
Must Have★★★★☆
Computer Networks · ~8–10 Marks
Computer Networking: A Top-Down Approach
Kurose & Ross
📌 Focus: Ch. 1 (Intro), Ch. 3 (Transport/TCP), Ch. 4-5 (Network/IP), Ch. 6 (Data Link). The subnetting questions require heavy practice with IP addressing — do lots of problems.
Must Have★★★★☆
Theory of Computation · ~6–8 Marks
Introduction to Automata Theory, Languages & Computation
Hopcroft, Motwani, Ullman
📌 Focus: Ch. 2 (DFA/NFA), Ch. 3 (Regular Expressions), Ch. 5 (CFG), Ch. 6 (PDA), Ch. 8-9 (Turing Machines, Undecidability). This book is dense — go slow.
Must Have★★★★★
Compiler Design · ~5–7 Marks
Compilers: Principles, Techniques & Tools (Dragon Book)
Aho, Lam, Sethi, Ullman
📌 Focus: Ch. 2-4 (Lexical Analysis, Parsing — LL/LR). The Dragon Book is advanced; for GATE, Ullman's lecture notes online are simpler. Parsing tables are frequently tested.
Selective Reading★★★★★
COA · ~5–7 Marks
Computer Organization & Architecture
William Stallings
📌 Focus: Pipelining, Cache Memory (mapping techniques), I/O (DMA, Interrupts). Numerical problems on cache hit/miss ratio are very common in GATE.
Recommended★★★★☆
Engineering Mathematics · ~13 Marks
Discrete Mathematics & Its Applications
Kenneth H. Rosen
📌 Focus: Ch. 1 (Logic), Ch. 5 (Induction), Ch. 8 (Relations), Ch. 10-11 (Graph Theory). For Probability, use Sheldon Ross "Introduction to Probability Models" (just Ch. 1-4).
Recommended★★★★☆
